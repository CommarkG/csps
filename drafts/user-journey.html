Context

B2B professional context — colleague mentions it, LinkedIn, or direct recommendation. The hook: "it captures your thinking and sorts it — you do nothing."

Alex's question

"I've tried these apps before and they always end up as graveyards. What's different here?"

Design implication

The app's market positioning must address graveyard anxiety directly. No onboarding screen says "organize your life." It says: "This takes 5 seconds. The app does the rest."

Day 0 — Onboarding
Three questions. No more.
Calibration, not setup

Question 1 — Role calibration

"What describes your work best?" → Options drive category weighting. A lawyer's "Act" bucket behaves differently than a founder's.

Question 2 — Capture preference

"When something crosses your mind, do you usually speak it or type it?" → Sets default input mode. This is the only UX preference we ask upfront.

Question 3 — Entry friction

"What's one thing that slipped through the cracks recently?" → Free-form voice. Seeds the first capture. Also teaches Alex the UX by doing it.

The transformation

Alex stops worrying about forgetting things. The physical sensation of "I need to write that down" is replaced by a quick voice note, no friction.

Architectural positions — OPUS-6 (react and redirect)

Sort model: Act / Decide / Delegate / Someday is a GTD-derivative. The risk is that knowledge workers know GTD and have already failed with it. The differentiator must be "you never manually assign a bucket" — the AI categorization must be accurate enough to feel magical, not like a smarter form.

Voice-first constraint: If the app is voice-first but the first-value moment requires Alex to open the app and see the inbox — that's a context switch. Consider: does the first-value moment arrive as a notification? "3 things sorted while you were in your meeting" could be higher-leverage than "open the app to see it."

Week-1 pattern surfacing: The pattern alert ("you mentioned X 6 times") is the sleeper feature. This is what makes the app feel intelligent rather than merely organized. It should appear earlier — Day 3, not Week 1 — to shorten the trust-building arc.
